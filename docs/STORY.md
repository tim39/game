# Story

> **A draft for the owner to rewrite.** Names, genders, ages, the plot and even the title are a starting proposal. Claude: don't change story canon without asking, but do point out plot holes.

## Logline

When the Beacon above a fishing village goes dark, apprentice lamplighter Rowan discovers that the sacred lights holding back the memory-eating Gloam run on something terrible, and that saving everyone may cost Rowan every memory of the journey.

## The world

**Aurel** lives in the light of four great **Beacons**: Tide, Gale, Stone and Ember. Beyond their glow lies **the Gloam**, a grey mist that eats memory. People lost in it forget who they are and become **the Hollowed**, the monsters that roam the dark.

The **Order of Wardens** tends the Beacons from the capital, Wardenhold. Every year at the **Kindling** festival, each town offers its Beacon a small memory: a song, a smell, a favorite day. Everyone thinks it's symbolic.

It isn't. The Beacons burn memory for fuel. When offerings fall short they take more, and sometimes a whole person vanishes. When that happens the world forgets them, as if they never existed. Only someone who was touching them at that moment still remembers.

The Gloam is the smoke of everything the Beacons have burned, and it feeds **the Hollow**, an ancient hunger sealed beneath Aurel. The cure feeds the disease, and the Order has always known.

## Plot

### Act 1: Embers (about 1–1.5 hours, levels 1–6)

1. **Saltmere, on Kindling day.** A tutorial walk: light the village lamps for the festival (teaching movement and interaction). Bram arrives to inspect the Tide Beacon. At the festival, villagers offer memories to the flame. Rowan offers a small one and, afterwards, can't remember what it was.
2. **The Beacon goes out.** That night the lighthouse goes dark, the Gloam rolls in and the Hollowed attack. A tutorial battle alongside Bram.
3. **Tide Caves** (dungeon 1). Down through the sea caves under the lighthouse to the Beacon chamber. *Gimmick:* switches raise and lower the tide to open paths. *Boss:* **the Drowned Warden**, a Hollowed knight. The Beacon is dead, its bowl full of black ash, with Vesh's sigil beside it. Its last spark leaps into Rowan (**Tide Edge**).
4. **Overworld, then Wardenhold.** A mini-boss on the road. In the capital, High Warden Aldis names Vesh a traitor who is snuffing Beacons and sends the party to guard the Gale Beacon. **Liora joins.**

### Act 2: The Beacons (about 2–2.5 hours, levels 7–20)

5. **Gale Spire** (dungeon 2). A tower on the sea cliffs. *Gimmick:* wind currents carry you along set paths. The party runs into Cass, who is there to steal the heartstone for Vesh's people. *Boss:* **the Ashen Knight**, Vesh's lieutenant, who betrays Cass. The Beacon holds, and its keeper gives Rowan a spark (**Gale Edge**). **Cass joins**, "for now".
6. **Deepholm and the Stone Deeps** (dungeon 3). The mining town of the Stonewrights, who built the Beacons. *Gimmick:* push boulders and ride mine carts. In the deepest vault, ancient murals show what the Beacons really burn. **This is the midpoint twist.** *Boss:* **the Vault Colossus**. Rowan gains **Stone Edge**. Liora is shaken.
7. **Wardenhold, again.** Aldis admits it: the Beacons are the price of light. The party walks out on the Order, and Bram chooses them over it.
8. **Ember Caldera** (dungeon 4), by way of the outpost of Cinderwatch. Vesh waits at the last Beacon. *Gimmick:* lava channels and cooling switches. He tells the party his daughter's name; no one but him remembers her. *Boss:* **Vesh**. Even beaten, he snuffs the Ember Beacon, and Rowan catches its last spark (**Ember Edge**). **All four Beacons are dark, and the Gloam covers Aurel.**

### Act 3: The Fifth Flame (about 1–1.5 hours, levels 21–28)

9. **The world in Gloam.** The towns are tinted and fogged, and people are forgetting each other. New, harder encounters roam the overworld. The Memory Shard quests come to a head. Vesh, humbled, admits that starving the Hollow woke it instead, and tells of the **Fifth Flame**: a light the first Wardens abandoned because it can't be taken, only given.
10. **The Hollow Below** (dungeon 5). Back down through the Tide Caves, full circle, into the heart of the Gloam: a dream-maze stitched together from every place in the game (reused tilesets in inverted palettes). *Gimmick:* darkness; the party sees only by the light of Rowan's sparks. Echoes of the forgotten appear: Cass's sister, Vesh's daughter, and a face Rowan almost remembers.
11. **The Hollow.** A multi-phase final battle. To end it, Rowan kindles the Fifth Flame from the four sparks and a memory freely given: Rowan's own memories of the journey.
12. **Epilogue.** Spring in Saltmere. The forgotten come home. Rowan remembers nothing of the adventure. Three people knock at the door: "Hi. You don't know us yet. Let us tell you a story." Credits.

## Memory Shards (side content)

Memory Shards, fragments of things the Beacons burned, are scattered through the world. Returning one to the right person restores a forgotten memory (a short story vignette) and gives a reward. v1 has 5–8 shard quests, and each restored memory adds a line to the epilogue.

## Writing guide

- Earnest first, funny second. The characters are kind to each other even when they disagree.
- Dialogue boxes hold at most 3 lines. Measured with the game's font, wrapping real lines, a line holds about 43 characters beside a portrait (36 to 49, depending on the words) and about 53 without one (50 to 58): so roughly 130 characters a box for people, and 160 for signs and narration. Choices fit about 28 characters each. `npm run validate` checks every line and choice against the real font, down every branch, so a line that doesn't fit can't ship.
- Most lines fit in one box. Big speeches can run 3–4 boxes, never more.
- Every NPC line is flavor, a hint or a joke. NPCs get new lines after major story flags.
- Plant the twist early: festival dialogue, the murals, NPCs who "can't remember why they're sad".
- Names are short and easy to say. Aurel leans Anglo-Celtic; the Stonewrights lean Norse.
